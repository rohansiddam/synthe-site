Quick start

On your main branch, in the repo, run

npx synthe@latest init

Using Claude Code, Cursor or Codex? Tell it:

Set up Synthe in this repo. Run npx synthe@latest guide and follow it.

What you need

  • A GitHub repo you can admin, with your agent's system prompt in it, as its own file or a string in your code.
  • The GitHub CLI, signed in with gh auth login, and Node 20 or later.
  • An Anthropic API key. Synthe saves it as a repo secret when you set it up.

Set it up

On your main branch, in the repo, run:

$ npx synthe@latest init

Say what your agent should get better at, confirm the checks Synthe proposes, paste your key, and choose automatic merges or review. When the run ends, open the Synthe: prompt report issue in your repo.

Setup writes three files into your repo: synthe.json, .synthe/scenarios.jsonl, and the workflow at .github/workflows/synthe.yml. The loop runs from that workflow.

Automatic merges land on your main branch. If it deploys your app, choose review.

The loop

Synthe works in rounds. In each round it proposes a small change to your agent's prompt, tests the change, and keeps it only if it clearly wins. Anything unclear leaves your prompt exactly as it was.

  1. 1

    Your checks. You confirm the checks in plain language: what a good reply does, stated so it can be tested.

  2. 2

    Simulated messages. Synthe builds test messages like the ones your agent really gets, split into learning cases it can study and held-out cases it has never seen.

  3. 3

    Propose a change. Synthe writes a small change to your prompt, aimed at the goal you set.

  4. 4

    Test the change. The change is tested against your checks, on learned and held-out messages.

  5. 5

    Merge clear wins. A change merges only when it is clearly better: on your checks, on held-out cases, and with no new rule breaks.

  6. 6

    Revert losers. Kept changes stay under watch. If one later loses, Synthe reverts it on its own.

  7. 7

    The morning report. Every run ends with a report: what changed, what it scored, what it cost.

Checks

Checks are plain-language statements of what a good reply does. Synthe proposes them at setup, from the goal you state, and your team confirms them before anything runs. Nothing is tested against a bar you have not seen.

Every change Synthe proposes is scored against your checks, and a change merges only when it is better on your checks, better on held-out cases, and breaks no rules it did not break before.

Your checks live in synthe.json, next to guardrails: the rules a reply must never break. See synthe.json.

Scenarios

Scenarios are the simulated messages Synthe tests your agent on. They live in your repo at .synthe/scenarios.jsonl, committed at setup, so the test set is versioned with your prompt.

The set is split in two. Learning scenarios are the ones Synthe can study while it works. Held-out scenarios are kept back: the part of Synthe that writes a change never sees them, and they are used only to score it. A change has to win on messages it was not written against, not just on the ones it studied.

Commands

CommandWhat it does
npx synthe@latest initSet up Synthe in a repo
npx synthe@latest guideThe setup guide, written for a coding agent to follow
npx synthe statusShow where it is
npx synthe stopStop it. Nothing more runs or changes
npx synthe restartStart it again after a stop

You can keep editing your prompt. After Synthe changes it, pull before you edit.

synthe.json

synthe.json sits at the root of your repo, written at setup. It holds the settings a run follows:

  • Your checks and guardrails, in plain language (see Checks).
  • budget.max_usd: the cap on what a run can spend.
  • "share": what Synthe may share with the Synthe team. Set "share": "none" to turn sharing off.

When sharing is on, Synthe sends your prompt, your test cases and how the run goes. It never sends your code, and it removes keys, emails and phone numbers first.

Your API key is not in this file. Synthe saves it as a repo secret (SYNTHE_ANTHROPIC_KEY).

The morning report

When a run ends, Synthe opens an issue in your repo titled Synthe: prompt report. The report leads with how often your checks passed, then shows every round: what was tried, what it scored, and why it was kept or dropped.

Every round reports its cost alongside its result, so what you spend is never a surprise. A round that does not clear the bar ends on its own; nothing merges until a change is clearly better.

Stop or remove it

Run npx synthe stop at any time. To remove it, also delete synthe.json, .synthe/ and .github/workflows/synthe.yml, and the repo secrets Synthe saved (SYNTHE_EVENT_CODE and SYNTHE_TEAM_CODE, or SYNTHE_ANTHROPIC_KEY). To undo a change, click Revert on its pull request.
