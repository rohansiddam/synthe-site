How the check works

          
The receiver runs these checks in order and stops at the first failure. Each of the six attacks breaks a different link.

          
    
            
  1. 1.
    Sender in registry. The sender must be a known agent, so there is a key to check the signature against. Fails with unknown_sender.
    2. 
            
  2. 2.
    Signature valid. The signature is recomputed over the signed envelope and compared. Fails with signature_invalid.
    3. 
            
  3. 3.
    Evidence pinned. Every referenced file is hashed and compared to its pinned hash. Fails with evidence_hash_mismatch.
    4. 
            
  4. 4.
    Approvals cover actions. Approvals travel as separate signed credentials. Every declared action must sit inside one, and each credential's own signature is verified. Fails with approval_missing or approval_signature_invalid.
    5. 
            
  5. 5.
    Plan intact. The steps are hashed and compared to the committed plan hash. Fails with plan_tampered.
    6. 
            
  6. 6.
    Not a replay. The idempotency key must not have been seen before. Fails with duplicate_idempotency_key.
    7. 
          

          
Honest boundaries: the demo keys stand in for real keypairs, and HMAC-SHA256 stands in for the signature scheme in the spec. What this proves is the shape of the guarantee. Tamper-evident, not tamper-proof. The full threat model lives in the repo.
