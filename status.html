Limits and your data

What Synthe doesn’t do, and where your data goes.

Plain facts about the limits of what Synthe tests, what each mode sends where, and what this site collects. They describe synthe 1.11.0.

Status

Early, open, and run on your own key.

Stage
Early. Synthe is open source under Apache-2.0 at github.com/synthelabs/synthe.
Not yet
No hosted service, no SLA, no price list. You pay your model provider directly, on your own key. For enterprise use, talk to us.

Limits

What a win from Synthe does and doesn’t tell you.

Simulated tests
Prompt mode tests changes on simulated single messages, not real users or conversations, and an AI judge marks the replies. Generated test messages are not reviewed by a person. Check the results yourself.
No tools
Prompt mode runs only your system prompt. Your agent’s tools and actions are not run.
Your machine
Solve mode measures on your computer. A number that moved there may move differently in production.
Your measure
Solve mode is only as good as the command that scores it. A measure that misses what matters can be gamed: protected files, guards and held-back data make that harder, not impossible.
Coding agents
A coding agent that proposes changes runs as you. Synthe keeps the measure and held-back data out of its folder, not out of its reach. If that matters, run the agent in a sandbox.
What can change
Prompt mode changes only your prompt file. Solve mode changes only the files your problem file names, on a new branch, never your working tree. Improve mode changes your code, but never your tests, settings, CI or hidden files. Every change arrives as a branch or a pull request, and every one can be reverted.
Small steps
A prompt-mode change is one or two edits. In Solve mode, you choose the files and the budgets. Big redesigns are not what it does.
Auto mode
In auto mode, a win merges into your default branch. If that branch deploys your app, the change is live. Choose review to approve each one.
Costs
With Anthropic, four models have known costs (Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 4.5). With another provider, Synthe uses the prices you give, or the cost the provider reports. If a prompt-mode round’s cost is unknown, it can’t merge and the run stops.
Attacks and guards
The attack messages are a basic check, not a security test. Guards are experimental: they make gaming the checks harder, not impossible.

Your data

What leaves your repo, and where it goes.

Where it runs
Prompt mode: setup on your computer, rounds in your repo’s GitHub Actions. Solve mode: on your computer, in a scratch checkout of your repo.
Your key
Prompt mode saves it as the repo secret SYNTHE_ANTHROPIC_KEY, or SYNTHE_API_KEY for another provider. Solve mode reads it from your shell. Model calls with your key go only to api.anthropic.com, or to the base_url you set for another provider. A coding agent as the proposer uses your own login, not a key.
To your model provider

Prompt mode: your prompt, goal, checks and rules, the test messages and your agent’s replies, as they are. At setup, also the start of your README and of the code file that uses your prompt, with text shaped like a key removed.

Solve mode: your goal, the files it may change, and the earlier attempts with their scores. Never held-back results. A coding agent as the proposer sees only the files it may change, and sends them to its own provider, on your login.

Improve mode: your goal, the commands it runs, your file names and sizes, and up to five files it chose to change, with text shaped like a key removed.

To the Synthe team

Only from prompt mode, and only what "share" in synthe.json allows:

numbers: each round’s result and cost, how often your checks passed, a score for each test message, why the run stopped, and the Synthe version. No text.

detail: the numbers, plus your goal, your checks, what each change did (its title, summary and up to two edits), and one example message with the replies before and after.

Keys, emails and phone numbers are removed first. No level sends your code. Each request carries a GitHub identity token for the run, which names your repo and commit, so we know which repo sent it. Solve mode and improve mode send the Synthe team nothing.

The default
New installs share detail: setup shows a notice and does not ask. Installs without a "share" setting share nothing.
Turning it off
Set "share": "none" in synthe.json at any time, or run setup with DO_NOT_TRACK=1 to start with sharing off.
Where it goes
To Synthe’s own server, at synthe-share.vercel.app, read only by the Synthe team. To have your team’s data deleted, open an issue on synthelabs/synthe.
In your repo
Synthe keeps its state on the synthe/state branch of your repo. Each round’s evidence file holds hashes and scores, not text. state.json holds text: your starting prompt, and each round’s edits and an example message with its replies. On a public repo, anyone can read this branch.

This site

What synthe.live itself does with your visit.

What it collects
Nothing on its own. No accounts, no analytics, no cookies, no tracking pixels.
Talk to us
Booking a call sends your name, email, company and note through our booking server, on Cloudflare, to our Google Calendar, which sends you the invite. The server keeps nothing. When you pick a time, the page loads Cloudflare Turnstile to check that you are a person. We use your details only for that call.

Security contact

Found a security problem in Synthe? Email rohansiddam@synthe.live first, and give us a chance to fix it before you publish. For anything else, GitHub issues are fine.
